Continuous Futures & Roll Handling

Part 1 on TY (10y UST) and RX (Euro-Bund) · Part 2 on STIRs · daily data 2020–2026, 22 roots

1. Roll definition

I roll on open interest, because it shows where positions actually sit. With s = OIfront / (OIfront + OInext), the roll date is the first of two consecutive sessions with s < 0.50, provided s was above 0.85 earlier in the cycle (a crossing, not a level). The thresholds act on a ratio, so the same numbers work on every bond root.

Alternatives. Volume is noisy (calendar spreads trade both legs) and misfires on 1–2 cycles on TY and RX. A calendar rule (first notice / last trading day) agrees with mine within 1 session on all 11 bond roots, but needs per-exchange rules that are not in the data, so I use it as a cross-check rather than the definition.

3. Continuous price and open interest

Price: difference (Panama) back-adjustment, judged against the true return of a held-and-rolled position. It keeps price changes exact, which is what matters for bonds; ratio adjustment keeps percentage returns exact but breaks when prices touch zero (crude printed −37.63 in April 2020). The unadjusted generic injects a fake 45 bp jump at every roll on TY (116 bp on RX).

Open interest: whole-curve aggregate OI. Generic-1 OI collapses to zero every quarter by construction; the aggregate does not care which contract is in front. Positioning is a z-score of log aggregate OI.

4. Robustness metric

Roll Artefact Score: standardise daily changes by normal-day volatility, then RAS = median |z| on roll days / median |z| on other days − 1, with a permutation p-value. RAS = 0 means roll days look like any other day.

5. Second bond future

RX and then all 11 bond roots ran with identical parameters: median roll of 3 sessions everywhere, calendar cross-check within 1 session, back-adjusted price clean on 10 of 11 roots (BTP is marginal because its roll gaps are the most volatile).

6. Part 2: short-term rate futures

"Front vs next" stops making sense: the front SOFR contract holds only ~11% of open interest, and it takes eight contracts to cover 75%. I would build constant-maturity series (interpolate between the two contracts around each target tenor, so there is no roll at all) and measure positioning as total strip OI plus its OI-weighted centre of mass along the curve, weighted by risk (DV01) rather than lot count.

7. Failure modes and production monitoring

  • Stale data: the provider sometimes freezes OI for days, which fakes a roll. Filtered; alert on runs of identical values.
  • Spread-out curves: on crude the rule fires 0 times in 82 cycles, because no contract dominates. Alert when a cycle reaches expiry with no roll.
  • Single-day roll gap: one noisy settlement distorts the adjustment. Alert on gaps beyond 3σ.
  • Spec or calendar changes: run the calendar rule in parallel and alert when the two disagree by more than 2 sessions.

8. With unlimited time

Build from individual contract histories instead of generics; roll over 3–5 days instead of one; validate the positioning series against CFTC Commitments of Traders; and test whether a signal on the adjusted series actually beats the raw generic out of sample, after costs.
